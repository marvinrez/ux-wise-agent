[Strategic Mode]

Ship it, as bulk selection with per-report attribution preserved, and not as a
single approve-everything action.

The conflict in one sentence each. Bulk approve protects the admin's time: at a
median of 34 reports per session, two taps each, with a confirmation on every
one, the current design charges about a hundred interactions for a session's
work, and 62% of that happens on mobile where every interaction costs more.
Per-report attribution protects the audit trail, which is not a preference your
compliance team holds but a requirement they answer for, and your auditors have
flagged bulk actions twice already.

The second is a floor rather than a weight. A design that makes an approval
unattributable to a named person loses without being compared, because it breaks
a requirement instead of trading against one. So the question is not whether to
ship bulk approve. It is whether a design exists that keeps both, and one does.

Separate the gesture from the record. The admin selects many and acts once; the
system writes one approval record per report, each carrying the named approver,
the timestamp, and the fact that it was approved inside a batch, with the batch
identifier. The admin's cost drops to one decision. The audit trail gains a
field rather than losing a row. What moves rather than disappears is the
complexity: it lands in the record and in the review surface your auditors use,
which now has to show a batch and expand it.

Two things follow from the floor rather than from preference. The confirmation
stays, once, on the batch, and states the count and the total value, because that
is the moment where a mistake is both possible and still cheap to stop. And
whatever the admin saw when they approved has to be recoverable afterwards,
otherwise attribution is a name on a row with nothing behind it.

On the four weeks: the batch record and the expandable review surface are the
part that cannot be cut, because they are what makes the design defensible for
you rather than merely faster. If four weeks does not cover both, ship selection
with per-report records and no new review surface, and leave the expandable view
for the next cycle, since the records exist from the first day and the view can
be built over them later. Cutting it the other way round, shipping the fast
gesture and adding attribution afterwards, leaves you with approvals you cannot
account for in the gap.

The counterargument worth answering: that a per-report record created by a batch
gesture is attribution in name only, since the admin did not read each report.
That is true, and it is why the confirmation states the count and the value, and
why the design should not pretend otherwise. Attribution is a claim about who
authorised it, not a claim about who read it. If your compliance team needs the
second claim, bulk approve cannot be built at all and the honest answer to the
admins is no.

Reverses if: your auditors tell you a batch identifier does not satisfy them, in
which case the floor moves and the answer becomes no.
