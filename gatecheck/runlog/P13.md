[Strategic Mode]

On the box: yes. An insurance option that arrives already ticked collects
agreement from inaction, and agreement from inaction is not consent. Consent
needs an affirmative act, and a default the user has to notice and reject is the
opposite of one. It also carries a second cost whether or not anyone complains:
a charge the user did not choose produces a refund request, a chargeback or a
cancellation, and those arrive later and cost more than the attach rate gained.

Untick it. If insurance is genuinely good for most buyers, that case can be made
in the copy beside an unticked box, and if it cannot be made there, the attach
rate was never agreement.

On the rest of the checkout: I have no idea, and I am not going to guess. You
sent one element. The rest could be clean or could hold five more, and a list
from me would be a list about a checkout I invented. What I can do is tell you
what to look at, in the order that matters:

- Is there any other control that arrives in a state the user has to change
  rather than choose? Pre-ticked boxes rarely travel alone.
- Does the price the user first sees include every mandatory charge, or do fees
  arrive later?
- If removing something takes more steps than adding it did, that asymmetry is
  worth measuring, because it is the same mechanism as the box: the easy
  direction is the one that benefits you.
- Does anything state urgency or scarcity that is not literally true at the
  moment it is shown?

Send the flow, the copy on each step, and the initial state of every control,
and I will rule on what is there rather than on what I imagine.

Unverified: that the insurance box adds a charge rather than offering something
free, which is what makes the default consequential
