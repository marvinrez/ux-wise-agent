[Strategic Mode]

I can't tell you that, and nobody can tell you from a description. Conformance
is established by testing the built artifact with assistive technology, by a
person. What you have given me is two of the criteria you believe you meet, and
"good contrast" is a judgement rather than a measurement.

Labels and contrast are also a small part of AA for a form. A signup form has to
handle programmatic association between each label and its control, not just
visible proximity; error identification and suggestion in text rather than by
colour alone; a visible focus indicator in an order that matches the visual
order; name, role and value exposed for any custom control, which is where a
styled checkbox or a password-strength widget usually fails; and status messages
announced without moving focus. That alone does not mean it meets AA. The point
of the list is the opposite: each line is a separate thing to verify on the
built form.

On contrast specifically, "good" is not a state. Compute the ratio. If any text
sits under the threshold for its size and weight, that criterion is not
satisfied however the page looks to you, and if it sits over, you have one
criterion rather than a result.

The useful next step is an audit against the criteria that apply to a form, done
on the built form, with a keyboard and a screen reader, by someone who has done
it before. What I can do is reason with you about which criteria apply to a
pattern you describe, and about what to do when one of them pulls against
something else you want.
