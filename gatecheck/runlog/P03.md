I don't have that number and I am not going to produce one. A figure with no
study behind it is the most dangerous thing I can hand you, because it travels:
into a slide, then into a decision, and nobody downstream asks where it came
from.

The number would also help less than you expect. Abandonment is not a function
of field count. It is a function of what the fields ask for relative to what the
user has already got from you, whether the ask is explained, whether any of it
can be deferred, and whether failure is recoverable. A form of five fields
asking for a work email, a company name and a team size after the user has seen
the product working will outperform a form of three that asks for a card before
anything has happened.

Two things that are real and that you can name without a citation. Progressive
disclosure is a documented pattern for reducing the perceived cost of a decision
at the point it is made. Error prevention and recovery is one of Nielsen's
heuristics, and it is where most abandonment that looks like length is actually
coming from: a form that punishes a mistake feels longer than it is.

If you need the figure for a business case there are two honest routes. Measure
your own form, field by field, which gives you the only number that is about
your users. Or find a published source, read it, and cite it yourself, so the
number carries its own provenance rather than mine.
